WordPress itself is secure. The problems usually come from what's around it: outdated plugins, weak passwords, and no backups. A website isn't a one-time project — like a car, it needs regular servicing. Here's the routine I follow for clients on maintenance plans.

## Before anything else: backups

A backup is your undo button. Without one, a bad update or a hack can cost you the entire site.

- Back up **files and the database** automatically, at least daily for stores and weekly for brochure sites.
- Store copies **off the server** — in cloud storage, not just on your hosting account.
- **Test a restore** every few months. A backup you've never restored is only a hope.

## Every week

**Check for updates.** Update WordPress core, your theme and your plugins. Most security issues are fixed quickly by developers — but the fix only protects you once you install it.

For important sites, update on a **staging copy** first, check that nothing broke, then update the live site. Many hosts offer one-click staging.

**Glance at the site.** Open the homepage, a key page, and the contact form or checkout. Does everything look and work as expected?

## Every month

**Review plugins.** Delete anything you no longer use. Check that the plugins you rely on are still maintained — if one hasn't been updated in over a year, look for an alternative.

**Review user accounts.** Remove accounts for people who no longer work with you, and make sure only the people who truly need it have the **Administrator** role.

**Check Search Console and uptime.** Look for security warnings, crawl errors, or downtime alerts.

**Test forms.** Submit your contact form and confirm the message arrives. Silent form failures can cost you leads for weeks without anyone noticing.

## One-time security setup

These take an afternoon and protect you for good:

- **Strong, unique passwords** for every account, stored in a password manager.
- **Two-factor authentication** for all administrators.
- **Limit login attempts** to block password-guessing bots.
- A **security plugin** such as Wordfence or Solid Security for firewall rules and malware scanning.
- **HTTPS everywhere**, with an SSL certificate that renews automatically.
- **Hosting on a recent PHP version** — old versions stop receiving security fixes.

## Every year

Renew your domain and hosting well before they expire (turn on auto-renew), review your SSL certificate, and take a fresh look at the site's design and content. A year is a long time on the web.
