I build on both WordPress and Wix, and clients often ask which one they should pick. There's no single right answer, but there is a right answer for *your* business. It mostly comes down to three questions: how much you'll grow, how much control you need, and who will maintain the site.

## The short version

| | **Wix** | **WordPress** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | Fast, everything included | Needs hosting, a theme and plugins |
| Monthly cost | Fixed subscription | Hosting + optional premium plugins |
| Design freedom | Good, inside Wix's editor | Unlimited, with custom code if needed |
| eCommerce | Fine for small catalogs | WooCommerce scales to large stores |
| SEO control | Solid basics | Full control over every detail |
| Moving away later | Hard — you can't export the site | Easy — you own every file |
| Maintenance | Wix handles it | You (or your developer) handle updates |

## When Wix is the better choice

Wix is a great fit when you want something live quickly and don't plan to grow it into a complex platform:

- A **portfolio, restaurant, or local service site** with a handful of pages.
- You want to **edit everything yourself** in a drag-and-drop editor.
- You don't want to think about hosting, updates, or security at all.

The trade-off is lock-in. Your site lives on Wix's servers, in Wix's format. If you outgrow it, you rebuild from scratch.

## When WordPress is the better choice

WordPress powers a huge share of the web for a reason. I recommend it when:

- You're building an **online store** that may grow to hundreds of products ([WooCommerce](https://woocommerce.com/) is free and extremely flexible).
- **SEO is a main sales channel** and you need control over URLs, schema, page speed and content structure.
- You need **custom features**: booking systems, member areas, multilingual content, product filters, integrations with your CRM.
- You want to **own your site** and be able to move hosts or developers at any time.

Most of the projects in [my portfolio](/projects) — like Factory Builder, Woodever and Relax Moment — are WordPress for exactly these reasons: rich product content, multiple languages, and room to grow.

## What about cost?

Wix looks cheaper at first because everything is bundled. WordPress costs more up front (design, development, setup) but usually less over time, because you aren't paying a premium subscription to unlock features, and you never pay to rebuild when you outgrow the platform.

A realistic way to compare is to estimate the **total cost over three years**, including the cost of a redesign if the platform can't keep up.

## My rule of thumb

- **Small, simple, and you want to DIY?** Start with Wix.
- **A business that depends on its website for leads or sales?** Build on WordPress.
